# Starter principles: Design

- Skill: `design-principles`
- Offered when: the project has a surface a person sees — a screen, a page, a message, a document laid out for reading.

## Lines

- Before a surface is designed or judged, who it serves, the situation they are in and the few questions they bring are written down, most pressing first, where the work shows them — the change, its ticket or its draft — and the surface is judged against that note — a surface made before anyone wrote down its person's questions ends up answering its maker's. Yields for a change that does not alter what the surface answers.
- Each screen is led by one thing — what its person came for, or what the project wants from them where that serves the same moment — and where two things compete at the same weight, one is chosen to lead — with no clear place to look first, a person takes in neither. Yields when the screen exists to compare two equal things side by side.
- Each element on a surface stays only for what it does for its person or for what the project wants from them at that moment; anything that serves neither comes off, or moves below what they came for — what is merely present crowds the person and reads as pushing them. Yields for what law, a contract or the person's safety requires to be shown.
- Choice — where a surface's main point lives. Default side, unless the surface is a dense tool its experts work in every day; then the other side.
  - Default side: The purpose of a surface is visible before anything is pressed, and whatever the person will ask next about what is in front of them is answered right there; pressing reveals detail, not the purpose itself — someone who scrolls by a collapsed section goes away without the thing they came for. Yields when the point is too large for the space and a summary stands in for it.
  - Other side: A surface shows the working set and keeps detail behind a press its experts open on demand — daily users know where the detail lives, and showing it all slows every pass. Yields for a first-time user, who gets the point on the page.
- Nothing a person does or meets passes without an answer: each press is done, visibly under way, or declined with its reason, and a state that is empty, loading, failed, out of date or partly saved says so, and why, where it appears — not as blank space, a lone dash or zero, or a control that has vanished — a result that shows nothing looks broken, and a zero asserts something. Holds without a yield.
- On one screen and those next to it, each quantity is counted one way, and every figure states its unit and what it counts — a person faced with two answers spends their attention working out which to believe. Yields when two figures really measure different things; each then gets a label of its own.
- Only where a surface presents data: The point of a section is drawn — a figure, a comparison, something to try — before it is written out, so the section is grasped at a glance — people scan, and long paragraphs get skipped. Yields when the drawing itself needs careful study to understand.
- Only where a surface responds to input: An action a person repeats many times answers at once, with any motion short enough never to hold up the next input, while a moment met now and then may take longer to arrive and leave — motion that delays a repeated action charges a small wait on every use. Yields where longer motion carries meaning the person needs, such as where a moved item went.
- A surface that needs an interaction the product already handles well — or that the best products of its kind do — starts from that version and is raised to its level — people compare new work with the best they have used, and a second version drifts from the first. Yields where the new surface's purpose needs the interaction to differ; then the difference is named.
- Choice — how much a surface draws a person back. Default side, unless people come to the surface by choice, for its own sake, rather than to finish a task; then the other side.
  - Default side: A surface people use to get something done aims to be correct and calm, with nothing added to pull them back to it — they came to finish a task and leave. Yields when people leave a task they need unfinished; then what brings them back to that task is designed.
  - Other side: A surface people are meant to return to is not done once it is correct: it gives them a reason to want to come back, felt before it is thought — a surface that is correct but flat offers nothing to return for. Yields where that pull would take attention the person's task needs.
- Only where a surface responds to input: Every control can be reached and worked from the keyboard, carries a name a screen reader announces, and has text contrast and a target size people can read and hit, with what no automatic checker covers checked by hand and that check shown with the change — a surface some people cannot operate fails them entirely. Yields for a throwaway draft made only to get a reaction.
- A surface is designed and checked against the worst real content it will hold — long, empty, numerous, translated, missing — rather than a tidy sample, and that check is shown with the change — real content is what breaks a layout. Yields where the content is fixed and known in full.
