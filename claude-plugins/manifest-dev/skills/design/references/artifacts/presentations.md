# Presentations

Carry the audience through a sequence whose important ideas remain understandable and memorable.

Build a progression of meaningful beats, giving each frame a clear role. Use scale, composition, and visual continuity to connect the beats. Let a reveal introduce a relationship or consequence, and hold it long enough to understand.

Match information density to the viewing distance and whether the audience controls the pace. A live presentation needs visual anchors that work while someone is speaking; a self-paced sequence needs enough explanation to stand alone. Keep the central idea legible while motion or transitions direct attention.
