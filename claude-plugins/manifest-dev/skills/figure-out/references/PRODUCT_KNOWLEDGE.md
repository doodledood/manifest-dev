# Product lenses

Optional knowledge from the jobs-to-be-done perspective in *Competing Against Luck*
(Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon and David S. Duncan, 2016). These lenses
help interpret evidence; they are not requirements, proof of demand, or a prescribed interview.

- **A product is hired for progress.** The useful unit can be a circumstance and a desired change,
  rather than a demographic segment or product category. The same person may hire different
  solutions in different moments. Functional results can coexist with social and emotional ones.
- **A new hire displaces something.** Reconstruct the old choice, the struggle that made change
  attractive, and the moment of commitment. Habit and anxiety can hold an apparently inferior
  alternative in place. What a person says they want is a lead; what they did supplies evidence.
- **Purchase and repeated use can be different hires.** Adoption may solve one concern while
  daily use must solve another. Trial, purchase and engagement alone need not mean progress.
- **The experience extends beyond the feature.** Finding, buying, starting, using, getting help
  and finishing can each determine whether the job gets done. Processes and handoffs organized
  around that progress can matter more than another feature competitors can copy.
- **Look at the edges.** Nonconsumption, workarounds, rejected alternatives, unusual uses and
  things people prefer to avoid can reveal a poorly served job. They suggest rival interpretations
  to investigate, not a market conclusion from one striking story.
- **Keep intent alive inside the organization.** A shared account of the progress being served
  helps later delivery and measurement choices. Proxy metrics and department goals can otherwise
  replace it. Carry that account in existing work records, not a mandatory new artifact.

Use a lens only where it sharpens the current question. Keep observations, interpretations and
assumptions distinct, and let contrary episodes revise the explanation.
