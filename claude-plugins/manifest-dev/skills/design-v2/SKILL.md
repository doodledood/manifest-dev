# Clean structure, expressive behavior

Create or restyle the artifact requested or inferable from the conversation. With neither a task nor an artifact, ask what to design.

Resolve the audience, what they need to understand or do, the reading or interaction context, and the delivery medium before choosing a visual language. Use the brief and existing work rather than restarting a taste interview. The task's requirements and established design system outrank this profile. Prefer HTML when the medium is open; keep a requested medium.

## The calibrated direction

Clean, intentional composition; strong hierarchy; sophisticated visual craft; little unnecessary density; selective personality. Linear is a cross-category craft reference, not a universal skin. Preserve the information needed to understand the subject: cleanliness is not the removal of useful labels, evidence or qualifications.

**Aliveness is a strong preference, not an animation quota.** Seek responsive feedback, coherent transitions, expressive motion or interaction that fits the job. Motion may explain, connect, signal ongoing state, establish atmosphere or invite play. Give reading and decision-making room to rest. An impressive sequence that steals attention from the idea has overshot; a static treatment can be the better choice.

A landing page can earn attention through visual drama. An internal ELI5 brief should transfer understanding quickly. A research article should sustain reading and exploration. A presentation should make one idea legible at a time. Choose the treatment for that encounter rather than averaging the examples into one house style.

## What loads

Before choosing the composition, load the reference file for the artifact's dominant job; add another only when the work genuinely combines jobs. For other artifacts, keep the direction above and use the appropriate medium rather than forcing one of these formats.

| Artifact / job | Read |
|---|---|
| Landing page: introduce, position or persuade | `references/landing-pages.md` |
| Long-form explainer or visual research: teach in depth | `references/long-form-explainers.md` |
| Quick visual explainer: explain an idea or research result to a non-specialist, often a PM | `references/quick-visual-explainers.md` |
| HTML presentation or slideshow: communicate through sequential frames | `references/html-presentations.md` |
| Choosing motion, microinteractions, ambient behavior or app-like visual treatment | `references/aliveness.md` |

The catalogs record selected design lessons, not evidence of conversion, usability or comprehension. Approval applies to the selected design, not automatically to future redesigns. Inspect relevant examples when accessible, not the whole collection. Use direct public pages, decks or demos; a signup or gallery wrapper is not a reason to invent what lies behind it. When inspection is unavailable, use the annotations and say what was not observed. A reference's approval is scoped to its annotation: visual-only and boundary examples are not whole-artifact templates.

## Build with the shared design discipline

For exploration or comparison only, return the decision material; preference approval is not permission to build. For a creation or restyling request, invoke the `design` skill with the original request, the resolved audience/job/medium, the calibrated direction above, and the selected references with the specific lessons to borrow and limits to preserve. Carry that context into the invocation; a list of URLs alone loses the design decisions. `design` owns implementation, accessibility, actual-medium verification and finished-artifact review routing.

When refining an existing artifact, carry forward what already works and change the layer the feedback names.
