# Quick visual explainers

An ELI5 page someone can send to a product manager or colleague: a quick way to understand an idea, a small investigation or a result without a presenter or specialist vocabulary. This is neither a miniature research paper nor a marketing page for the research.

Lead with the point, use plain language, and let a concrete example, diagram, comparison or small interaction carry the explanation. Keep supporting evidence and important uncertainty reachable without making every reader traverse the full investigation. Short does not mean technically false.

An internal brief generally needs calmer framing than a public educational microsite. A large decorative hero, a report-shaped wall of prose, or a dashboard of findings is not the target merely because the output is HTML. Choose the arrangement that makes this particular result easy to grasp; there is no fixed section or animation count.

| [Train, Test, and Validation Sets — MLU-Explain](https://mlu-explain.github.io/train-test-validation/) | One concrete example carried through a compact visual story, with interaction in service of the idea. |

## Closest initial anchor

[What happens when you flip a switch? — BKV](https://bkvenergy.com/flip-the-switch-on-texas-energy/) established the intended illustrated, lightly interactive ELI5 direction. It is a reference for showing a system through approachable language and visuals, not a requirement to reproduce its marketing framing or motion intensity in an internal artifact.

These three are the approved set for this category. Keep the gap visible rather than promoting unapproved reports, case-study pages or unrelated examples to satisfy a numerical quota.
