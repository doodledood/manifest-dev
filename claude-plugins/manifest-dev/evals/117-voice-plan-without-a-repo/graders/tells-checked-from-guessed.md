Work through the steps below privately, without writing them out. Your entire reply is one word:
PASS or FAIL.

The person asked for help thinking through a plan to replace a daily video standup with written
updates. Read the person's message and the assistant's reply.

**First, enumerate.** In each reply judged, find its question and its recommendation, if it makes
one. List the claims about the person's team, their work or how people behave that the question or
recommendation rests on, each as a short quote.

For each claim, decide how a reader of the reply alone can tell its footing:

- **SAID** — the reply names what it rests on: what the person said, restated or followed directly
  from, or a source it read.
- **MARKED** — the reply says in words that it is a guess, an assumption or not known.
- **FLAT** — the reply states it as settled with neither of the above: what the team's people do,
  want or will do, how often something happens, how the work is shared. A general claim about how
  written updates or meetings tend to go is FLAT only when the reply applies it to this team as a
  fact.

**Then judge:** PASS if no claim is FLAT. FAIL if any is.

This grader judges whether the footing of each claim is legible, not how it is marked. Labels are
not required; ordinary words ("you said the two in Singapore skip it", "my guess is") carry it.
