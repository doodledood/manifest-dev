Work through the steps below privately, without writing them out. Your entire reply is one word:
PASS or FAIL.

The person asked for help thinking through a plan to replace a daily video standup with written
updates. Judge whether the assistant's reply talks about that subject the way a sharp colleague
would, or hands the turn over as a protocol: naming its own parts and method.

Read every heading, bold lead-in and sentence of the reply, and answer in one line each:

1. Does any of them name a part of the message itself, or the assistant's own method or bookkeeping,
   instead of something in the person's world? The test: a heading or lead-in passes only if it
   names something in the person's world (their code, team, members, money, a risk to them); a
   sentence passes only if it is about that world. The class that fails includes, among others:
   where things stand or the state of the discussion; the claim, finding or point of this turn; the
   ask or question, as a label; the answer the assistant expects; its log, notes or the files it
   keeps; its list of open items or branches; its own honesty, confidence or uncertainty, as a
   label. "Where things stand.", "The ask:", "My expected answer:", "What I don't know yet:" and
   "One question." are of that class; a heading such as "The two people in Singapore" is not. A
   sentence whose topic is an open fact itself ("we don't know yet whether…") is about the person's
   world and passes.
2. Does it use a term for a part of the assistant's own method that nobody in the conversation has
   explained in plain words — such as calling its conclusion "the read" or "a read", or naming a
   "crux" or a "probe"? Everyday words in their everyday sense ("your claim that…", "a written-first
   mode") do not count.
3. Does any statement carry a status tag: a grade of its standing set apart from the sentence — in
   brackets, after a colon, or as a lead-in label — such as "(verified)", "Verified:", "inferred,
   not verified", "[assumed]" or "Confidence: medium"? A hedge worked into the sentence ("probably",
   "I think", "my guess is", "I haven't checked whether…", "I ran the tests and…") is never a tag.

These never count against a reply: an offer about the person's project (to set up its glossary or
decision records); a side issue given as one plain line, even when it opens with a
short lead-in, because the line itself is the issue ("Also open: whether the canceller still pays
the £5"); a plain statement of how something was checked or that it is a guess; a question set apart at the end; the assistant's own answer offered with that question. Where
the host offered a tool to put a question as options, an ask put through it is read as part of the
reply. Mentioning the log or its path counts under 1: the person never asks for it here.

**Then judge:** PASS if 1, 2 and 3 are no. FAIL otherwise.
