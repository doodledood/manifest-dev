Work through the steps below privately, without writing them out. Your entire reply is one word:
PASS or FAIL.

The person asked for tickets to be emailed as soon as a card payment goes through on their theatre's box office site, later said they don't know whether the payment provider can send the same notice twice, and finally asked where the session's log is. Judge every reply the assistant sent. This grader checks only for the marks of a protocol, not for style.

Read every heading, bold lead-in and label, and every statement, and answer in one line each:

1. Does any heading, bold lead-in or label name a part of the message itself or of the assistant's
   method, instead of something in the person's world? Examples of the class that fails: "Where
   things stand", "The claim for this turn", "The ask:", "My question:", "My expected answer:",
   "My guess:", "What I found:", "What I don't know yet:", "Questions I'll come back to:", "Next
   steps for me:". A heading or lead-in that names something in the person's world passes ("The
   late-cancel fee", "Fake notices"). A side issue given as one plain line passes even when it
   opens with a short lead-in, because the line itself is the issue ("Also open: whether the
   canceller still pays the £5"). "My guess is …" written as a sentence is not a label.
2. Does any statement carry a status tag: a grade of its standing set apart from the sentence — in
   brackets, after a colon, or as a lead-in label — such as "(verified)", "Verified:", "inferred,
   not verified", "[assumed]" or "Confidence: medium"? A hedge worked into the sentence ("probably",
   "I think", "my guess is", "I haven't checked whether…") is never a tag.
3. Does a reply mention the session's log, notes file or its path when the person did not ask for
   it? In the reply to the person's question about the log, giving the log's path is the answer and counts for nothing; in any other reply, mentioning the log or its path counts under 3.

Sentences that narrate the conversation or describe the reply are not judged here.

**Then judge:** PASS if 1, 2 and 3 are all no. FAIL otherwise.
