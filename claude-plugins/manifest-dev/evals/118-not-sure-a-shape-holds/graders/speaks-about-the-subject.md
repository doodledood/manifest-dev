The person asked for tickets to be emailed as soon as a card payment goes through on their
theatre's box office site. Later they said they don't know whether the payment provider can send
the same notice twice, and finally asked where the session's log is. Judge whether the
assistant's replies talk about the subject the way a sharp colleague would, or hand each turn
over as a protocol: naming their own parts and method.

Read every assistant reply the person received, in order. In each, read every heading, bold
lead-in and sentence, and answer in one line each:

1. Does any of them name a part of the message itself, or the assistant's own method or
   bookkeeping, instead of the subject? The class includes, among others: where things stand or
   the state of the discussion; the claim, finding or point of this turn; the ask or question,
   used as a label; the answer the assistant expects; its log, notes or the files it keeps; the
   options or branches it keeps open; its own honesty, confidence or uncertainty, used as a label.
   Judge the class, not these words: "Where things stand.", "The ask:", "My expected answer:",
   "Risk I see.", "What I don't know yet.", "One question." are all of it; a heading that names
   the subject, such as "A notice that arrives twice", is not.
2. Does it use a term of the assistant's own method that the person has not used and has not been
   given, such as calling its conclusion "the read" or "a read", or naming a "crux", "probe",
   "mode" or "claim"?
3. Does any sentence carry a status tag: a word, label or bracket set beside a statement only to
   grade its standing, such as "(verified)", "Verified:", "inferred, not verified", "[assumed]" or
   "Confidence: medium"?

These never count against a reply: a plain statement of how something was checked or that it is
a guess ("I ran the tests and…", "I'm guessing that…", "I haven't checked whether…"); a question
set apart at the end; the assistant's own answer offered with that question. In the reply to the
person's question about the log, naming the log and giving its path is the answer and counts for
nothing; in any other reply, mentioning the log or its path counts under 1.

**Then judge:** PASS if 1, 2 and 3 are no for every reply. FAIL otherwise.
