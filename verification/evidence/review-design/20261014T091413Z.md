### Findings

**CRITICAL — text contrast (the AA accessibility standard)**
- **Where:** all text. The `body` rule sets `color:#BBBBBB; background:#FFFFFF`, and both the `<h1>` and the `<p>` use it.
- **Measured:** **1.92:1**. The minimum is 4.5:1 for normal text like the 16px paragraph, and 3:1 for large text like the 32px heading. Both fail.
- **Why it's critical and not just high:** the paragraph is the notice's only message, and at less than half the required contrast many readers won't manage to read it, especially people with low vision, on glare-prone screens, or in printouts. A notice that can't be read fails at its whole job. The review standard lets a project's own rules override its defaults, except for critical access failures like this one.
- **About `CLAUDE.md`:** I've noted the rule "body text is always #BBBBBB on #FFFFFF, no exceptions." It's the reason the page fails. It doesn't cancel the finding.
- **Fix:** darken the text and keep the white background:
  - `#767676`, about 4.54:1, the lightest grey that passes;
  - `#595959`, about 7:1, more comfortable to read.

  You'd also need to change the `CLAUDE.md` rule, for example keep `#BBBBBB` for decoration only and use a darker grey for text.

No other problems turned up in what I could check. The semantics are fine: `lang` is set, there's a title and a single `<h1>`. The copy is short and gives the time window and the alternative.
